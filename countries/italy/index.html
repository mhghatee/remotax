Statutory Tax System Overview (2026)

Under the Italian Consolidated Income Tax Act (Testo Unico delle Imposte sui Redditi - TUIR), tax residency is established when an individual is registered in the resident civil registry (Anagrafe) or maintains habitual abode or their center of vital interests in Italy for more than 183 days in a fiscal year. The national personal income tax (Imposta sul Reddito delle Persone Fisiche - IRPEF) is structured under three progressive statutory brackets as codified in the Legge di Bilancio 2026. The initial bracket taxes taxable earnings up to €28,000 at 23%; the middle bracket taxes income between €28,000 and €50,000 at 33%; and the top bracket levies 43% on taxable amounts surpassing €50,000.

Social Security & Mandatory Deductions

Social security obligations in Italy are administered by the Istituto Nazionale della Previdenza Sociale (INPS). For salaried remote employees, standard employee pension contributions are levied at 9.19% up to the first pensionable threshold of €56,224, plus an additional 1% (10.19%) on excess remuneration up to the statutory contribution ceiling (massimale contributivo ex art. 2 c. 18 Legge 335/1995). For the 2026 tax year, pursuant to Circolare INPS n. 6/2026, this ceiling is established at €122,295, capping maximum annual employee social security at €11,899.62. For independent remote contractors and freelance consultants who do not belong to a dedicated professional order (professionisti senza cassa), contributions must be remitted to the INPS Separate Management scheme (Gestione Separata). Under Circolare INPS n. 8/2026, the Gestione Separata rate is 26.07% applied to net professional earnings up to the identical €122,295 massimale ceiling, fixing the maximum statutory annual contractor contribution at €31,882.31.

Special Expatriate Tax Regimes

The Regime Lavoratori Impatriati underwent major structural codification under Article 5 of Legislative Decree 209/2023. Eligible international workers relocating their tax residency to Italy benefit from a 50% statutory exemption on qualifying employment and self-employment income, effectively reducing their national IRPEF exposure by half. This exemption increases to 60% for professionals relocating with an accompanying minor child or purchasing a primary residential property in Italy. The benefit applies up to an annual gross earnings ceiling of €600,000 for a duration of 5 consecutive tax years. Qualifying criteria require that the worker was not tax resident in Italy in the 3 preceding tax years, possesses high professional qualifications, and commits to maintaining statutory Italian tax residency for at least 4 continuous years.

Frequently Asked Questions: Italy Taxes (2026)

What are the rules for Italy's Regime Impatriati under Decree 209/2023 in 2026?

Under Legislative Decree 209/2023 (Art. 5), qualifying incoming workers receive a 50% tax exemption on Italian-assessed income up to €600,000 per year for 5 years (60% if moving with a minor child). Eligibility requires being a tax non-resident of Italy for the prior 3 tax years, having high professional/technical qualifications, and committing to maintain Italian residency for at least 4 tax years. Breaching the stay requirement triggers retroactive clawback with penalties.

How does INPS social security work for remote contractors in Italy in 2026?

Freelance contractors and digital nomads without an Italian professional register pay into Gestione Separata at 26.07%. Contributions are strictly capped at the statutory massimale of €122,295 (under Circolare INPS 8/2026), meaning income above €122,295 pays zero additional social security, capping the annual contribution at €31,882.31.

What is the difference between Regime Impatriati and the 7% Pension Regime in Italy?

Regime Impatriati is designed for active remote workers, employees, and freelance contractors providing a 50% earned income exemption. The 7% Flat Tax (Art. 24-ter TUIR) applies exclusively to retirees holding foreign pensions who relocate to municipalities with under 20,000 residents in designated southern regions.

Can international remote employees use Italy's Digital Nomad Visa?

Yes. Italy's Digital Nomad Visa permits highly qualified non-EU remote employees and freelancers to legally reside in Italy. Requirements include proof of minimum annual income (around €28,000), health insurance, suitable accommodation, and at least 6 months of documented remote professional experience.

Are foreign assets and bank accounts taxable in Italy?

Standard Italian tax residents must disclose foreign financial assets on Form RW and pay IVAFE (0.20% on foreign financial accounts) and IVIE (on foreign real estate). However, electing certain high-net-worth flat tax regimes (such as the €200,000 lump-sum regime under Art. 24-bis TUIR) fully replaces foreign asset taxation.
