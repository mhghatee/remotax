Key Requirements for the Portugal D8 Nomad Visa

To qualify for Portugal's D8 Digital Nomad Visa in 2026, foreign remote workers must demonstrate:

  • Statutory Minimum Income: At least 4 times the Portuguese national minimum wage (~€3,280/month in 2026).
  • Proof of Remote Activity: Active employment contract with an overseas company or freelance service agreements (Recibos Verdes) with clients outside Portugal.
  • Accommodation & Insurance: Proof of long-term accommodation (12-month lease agreement) and valid comprehensive health insurance.

Tax Benefits: How the 20% Flat Rate Works (NHR 2.0 / IFICI)

While the original 2009 Non-Habitual Resident (NHR) program closed to new broad applicants in 2024, the Portuguese government introduced the Tax Incentive for Scientific Research and Innovation (IFICI) under Article 58-A of the Tax Benefits Code.

Eligible professionals engaged in qualifying technology, research, research-and-development, and innovative startup activities can secure a flat 20% personal income tax (IRS) rate on eligible labor and independent professional income for up to 10 consecutive years.

Segurança Social for Remote Workers

Under Category B (independent professional / Recibos Verdes), independent contractors in Portugal contribute to Segurança Social based on a deemed relevant income of 70% of professional services. After the initial exemption period for newly registered freelancers (often up to 12 months), the standard quarterly contribution rate is approximately 21.4% applied to the relevant deemed income, subject to monthly statutory floors and ceilings.

Portugal vs Spain: Which Yields Higher Disposable Income?

Portugal offers a lower baseline cost of living compared to Madrid or Barcelona, but Spain's Beckham Law provides a broader, simpler 24% flat rate up to €600,000 without requiring specialized scientific or startup qualification.
