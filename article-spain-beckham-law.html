Spain Beckham Law 2026: Complete Expat Tax Guide & Application Steps

Published by Remotax Research Team • Updated July 2026 • 8 Min Read

Spain has rapidly grown into one of Europe's top destinations for remote workers, software engineers, and digital nomads. However, standard Spanish personal income tax rates (IRPF) can reach up to 47% for high earners. Fortunately, foreign remote workers relocating tax residency to Spain can take advantage of a powerful tax incentive known as the Beckham Law.

What is the Beckham Law in Spain?

Formally known as the Régimen Especial para Trabajadores Desplazados (RETD), the Beckham Law is a special tax regime introduced to allow non-resident individuals relocating to Spain to pay personal income tax under non-resident rules. Originally enacted in 2005 when footballer David Beckham joined Real Madrid, recent legislative reforms under Spain's Startup Law (Ley de Startups) have expanded eligibility to include digital nomads, remote workers, and technology startup founders.

Key Tax Benefits of the Beckham Law

  • Flat 24% Income Tax Rate: Instead of progressive tax brackets ranging from 19% to 47%, your Spanish-sourced earned income up to €600,000 is taxed at a fixed flat rate of 24%. Earnings exceeding €600,000 are taxed at 47%.
  • Exemption on Foreign Income & Assets: Unlike standard Spanish tax residents who are taxed on worldwide income, Beckham Law beneficiaries are only taxed on earned income generated in Spain. Foreign investment income, capital gains, and bank interest earned abroad are completely exempt.
  • No Form 720 Filing Requirement: Standard Spanish tax residents must file Form 720 (Modelo 720) disclosing all foreign assets valued over €50,000. Under Beckham Law, you are exempt from Form 720 asset reporting.
  • 6-Year Duration: The regime applies during the calendar year of relocation plus the following 5 consecutive tax years (total of 6 years).

Who Qualifies for the Beckham Law in 2026?

To qualify for the Beckham Law, you must meet the following statutory requirements established by the Spanish Tax Agency (Agencia Tributaria):

  1. No Spanish Tax Residency in Prior 5 Years: You must not have been a tax resident in Spain during the 5 calendar years prior to your relocation.
  2. Valid Cause of Relocation: You must move to Spain under one of the approved categories:
    • An employment contract with a Spanish employer or Employer of Record (EOR).
    • Remote employment with a non-Spanish company (Digital Nomad Visa holder).
    • Becoming an administrator of a qualifying Spanish entity or startup.
  3. Strict Application Window: You must file Form 149 (Modelo 149) within 6 months of registering with Social Security or starting work in Spain. Missing this 6-month deadline permanently disqualifies you.

Step-by-Step Application Process (Modelo 149)

Follow these steps to submit your Beckham Law application directly to Agencia Tributaria:

  1. Obtain your Spanish NIE (Número de Identificación de Extranjero) and local town hall registration (Empadronamiento).
  2. Secure your Digital Certificate (Certificado Digital) or Cl@ve PIN to access Spanish government web portals.
  3. Complete and submit Modelo 149 online along with supporting documents (employment contract, Digital Nomad visa approval, passport copy).
  4. Receive official approval certificate (Certificado del Régimen Especial) from Agencia Tributaria (typically processed within 30 to 60 days).
  5. Notify your employer/EOR to adjust your monthly income tax withholding (IRPF) down to 24%.

📌 Important Advice for Digital Nomads

If you operate as a self-employed freelancer (Autónomo), ensure your digital nomad visa documentation specifically registers your activity under qualifying remote economic activity rules under the 2023 Ley de Startups amendments.
