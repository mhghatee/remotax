Spain has rapidly grown into one of Europe's top destinations for remote workers, software engineers, and digital nomads. However, standard Spanish personal income tax rates (IRPF) can reach up to 47% (and over 50% in regions like Catalonia). Fortunately, foreign remote workers relocating tax residency to Spain can take advantage of a powerful tax incentive known as the Beckham Law.

What is the Beckham Law in Spain?

Formally known as the Régimen Especial para Trabajadores Desplazados (RETD) under Article 93 of the Spanish Personal Income Tax Act, the Beckham Law is an expatriate tax regime that allows qualifying foreigners who move to Spain to be taxed under Non-Resident Income Tax rules (IRNR) while legally residing in the country.

Originally enacted in 2005 when footballer David Beckham joined Real Madrid, major statutory reforms under Spain's Startup Law (Ley de Startups) officially expanded eligibility to digital nomads, remote workers employed abroad, and startup executives.

Key Tax Benefits of the Beckham Law

  • Flat 24% Income Tax Rate: Instead of progressive tax brackets ranging from 19% to 47%, your Spanish-sourced earned income up to €600,000 is taxed at a fixed flat rate of 24%. Earnings exceeding €600,000 are taxed at 47%.
  • Exemption on Foreign Income & Assets: Unlike standard Spanish tax residents who are taxed on worldwide income, Beckham Law beneficiaries are only taxed on earned income generated in Spain. Foreign investment income, capital gains, and bank interest earned abroad are completely exempt.
  • No Form 720 Filing Requirement: Standard Spanish tax residents must file Form 720 (Modelo 720) disclosing all foreign assets valued over €50,000. Under Beckham Law, you are exempt from Form 720 asset reporting.
  • 6-Year Duration: The regime applies during the calendar year of relocation plus the following 5 consecutive tax years (total of 6 years).

Who Qualifies for the Beckham Law in 2026?

To qualify for the Beckham Law, you must meet the following statutory requirements established by the Spanish Tax Agency (Agencia Tributaria):

  1. No Spanish Tax Residency in Prior 5 Years: You must not have been a tax resident in Spain during the 5 calendar years prior to your relocation.
  2. Valid Cause of Relocation: You must move to Spain under one of the approved categories:
    • An employment contract with a Spanish employer or Employer of Record (EOR).
    • Remote employment with an overseas company (Digital Nomad Visa holder).
    • Becoming an administrator of an innovative Spanish startup company.
  3. Strict 6-Month Application Window: You must file Form 149 (Modelo 149) within 6 months of registering with Social Security or starting work in Spain. Missing this 6-month deadline permanently disqualifies you.

Step-by-Step Application Process (Modelo 149)

Follow these steps to submit your Beckham Law application directly to Agencia Tributaria:

  1. Obtain your Spanish NIE (Número de Identificación de Extranjero) and local town hall census registration (Empadronamiento).
  2. Secure your Digital Certificate (Certificado Digital) or Cl@ve PIN to access Spanish government web portals.
  3. Complete and submit Modelo 149 online along with supporting documents (employment contract, Digital Nomad visa approval, passport copy).
  4. Receive the official approval certificate (Certificado del Régimen Especial) from Agencia Tributaria (typically processed within 30 to 60 days).
  5. Notify your employer or payroll provider to adjust your monthly income tax withholding down to 24%.

Important Advice for Digital Nomads

If you operate as a self-employed professional (Autónomo), ensure your Digital Nomad Visa paperwork specifies qualifying remote activity under the amended Ley de Startups guidelines, as traditional retail/commercial freelancing does not automatically qualify.
