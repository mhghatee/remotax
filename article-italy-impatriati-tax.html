Italy Impatriati Tax Regime 2026: 50% to 70% Exemption Guide

Italy offers one of Europe's most aggressive statutory tax incentives for incoming high-skilled workers, corporate executives, and remote specialists. Under the Regime Impatriati (governed by Legislative Decree 147/2015 and updated by Legislative Decree 209/2023), qualifying individuals moving their tax residency to Italy can shield 50% to 60% of their gross income from personal income tax (IRPEF).

How the Regime Impatriati Operates

Rather than taxing your entire earnings under standard Italian IRPEF progressive brackets (which reach 43% on income over €50,000 plus regional and municipal surcharges), the regime subjects only 50% of eligible earned income to taxation. The remaining 50% is completely tax-free.

The 2024–2026 Legislative Reforms (Decree 209/2023)

For individuals establishing tax residency from 2024 onward, the Italian Parliament refined the regime parameters:

  • Baseline Exemption: 50% exemption on taxable employment and self-employment income.
  • Family Enhancement (60% Exemption): If the taxpayer relocates with a minor child or has a child during their Italian residence, the exemption increases to 60%.
  • Statutory Cap: The exemption applies to qualifying income up to a maximum ceiling of €600,000 per fiscal year. Income above €600,000 is taxed at standard IRPEF rates.

Eligibility & Qualification Requirements

To obtain approval from the Italian Revenue Agency (Agenzia delle Entrate), applicants must satisfy:

  1. Prior Non-Residency: You must not have been an Italian tax resident for the 3 consecutive tax years prior to relocation (or up to 6-7 years if moving within the same corporate group).
  2. Minimum Residency Commitment: You must commit to remaining an Italian tax resident for at least 2 consecutive tax years. Leaving before 2 years triggers retroactive clawback of all tax savings plus penalties.
  3. High Qualification Requirement: You must hold recognized academic or professional qualifications (university degree or regulated technical credential).

INPS Social Security Contributions

It is important to remember that the Impatriati exemption applies strictly to IRPEF income tax. Social security contributions (INPS Gestione Separata for freelancers, approx. 26.07%, or standard employee payroll contributions ~9.19%) are calculated on the full gross income basis up to statutory INPS contribution ceilings (~€113,520).

The Alternative: Italy's €100,000 Lump-Sum Expat Regime

For ultra-high-net-worth individuals with substantial foreign capital gains, dividends, or international business holdings, Italy's separate Article 24-bis lump-sum regime allows paying a single fixed flat tax of €100,000/year (recently updated to €200,000 for new entrants) on all non-Italian sourced income, regardless of total earnings.
