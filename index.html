Understanding Remote Worker Tax Residency (The 183-Day Framework)

Navigating cross-border taxes is the single most important financial step for any digital nomad or remote employee. While spending more than 183 days in a country within a tax year is the most common statutory threshold triggering local tax residency, it is not the sole test. Tax residency rules vary across jurisdictions and frequently evaluate your permanent home, center of vital interests (family, economic, and banking ties), and habitual abode.

Key Factors Determining Your Worldwide Tax Obligations:

  • Statutory Presence vs. Center of Vital Interests: Physical presence triggers domestic tax residency, but bilateral Double Tax Treaties (DTTs) employ "tie-breaker" rules to prevent dual taxation on the same income stream.
  • Visas vs. Tax Regimes: Holding a Digital Nomad Visa or residence permit does not automatically grant tax exemption. Tax liability is governed separately by domestic tax codes and special incentive programs (e.g. Beckham Law in Spain, IFICI in Portugal).
  • B2B Freelance vs. EOR Employment: Operating as a registered B2B contractor allows deduction of valid business overheads before computing taxable profit, whereas EOR employees are taxed via mandatory source withholding.

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about remote taxes and calculations.

How is the Beckham Law in Spain calculated?

Spain's Beckham Law allows non-residents relocating to Spain to pay a flat 24% income tax rate on earnings up to €600,000, instead of standard progressive tax rates that reach up to 47%. It also exempts foreign investment income from Spanish wealth tax.

What is the difference between B2B Contractor and EOR Employee?

A B2B contractor bills companies directly via invoice and manages their own social security and tax withholdings. An Employer of Record (EOR) acts as your legal local employer, withholding local taxes and social security automatically from your gross pay.

How does double taxation work for US Citizens working abroad?

US citizens are taxed on worldwide income regardless of where they live. However, tools like the Foreign Earned Income Exclusion (FEIE) and Foreign Tax Credits (FTC) generally eliminate or reduce US tax liability on income taxed in your host country.
