Understanding Remote Worker Tax Residency (The 183-Day Rule)

Navigating cross-border taxes is the single most important financial step for any digital nomad or remote employee. Most countries enforce the 183-day rule, meaning that if you spend more than 183 days within a calendar year in a single jurisdiction, you automatically become a tax resident subject to local income taxes on your worldwide income.

Key Factors Determining Your Tax Obligations:

  • Tax Residency vs. Physical Presence: Physical presence triggers tax liability, but double taxation treaties (DTTs) prevent paying tax twice on the same income.
  • B2B Freelance vs. EOR Employment: Operating as a B2B contractor usually allows you to deduct valid business expenses (laptops, co-working spaces, software) before computing taxable net pay.
  • Digital Nomad Visas: Countries like Spain, Portugal, Greece, and Dubai offer specific tax incentives to attract remote workers without burdening local labor markets.

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about remote taxes and calculations.

How is the Beckham Law in Spain calculated?

Spain's Beckham Law allows non-residents relocating to Spain to pay a flat 24% income tax rate on earnings up to €600,000, instead of standard progressive tax rates that reach up to 47%. It also exempts foreign investment income from Spanish wealth tax.

What is the difference between B2B Contractor and EOR Employee?

A B2B contractor bills companies directly via invoice and manages their own social security and tax withholdings. An Employer of Record (EOR) acts as your legal local employer, withholding local taxes and social security automatically from your gross pay.

How does double taxation work for US Citizens working abroad?

US citizens are taxed on worldwide income regardless of where they live. However, tools like the Foreign Earned Income Exclusion (FEIE) and Foreign Tax Credits (FTC) generally eliminate or reduce US tax liability on income taxed in your host country.
