Understanding Cross-Border Tax Residency (183-Day Framework)

Navigating cross-border obligations is the single most critical financial step for location-independent professionals. While spending more than 183 days in a country within a single tax year is the most common statutory threshold triggering domestic tax residency, it is not the sole test. Tax residency rules vary across jurisdictions and frequently evaluate permanent dwelling availability, center of vital economic interests, and habitual abode.

Statutory Tie-Breaker Rules & Double Tax Treaties (DTT)

Under OECD Model Tax Conventions, if you qualify as a tax resident in two countries simultaneously, bilateral double tax treaties employ hierarchy tests: permanent home first, followed by center of vital interests (family, economic, and banking ties), nationality, and mutual agreement between tax authorities.
