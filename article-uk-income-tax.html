The United Kingdom maintains a transparent HMRC framework for PAYE employees and self-employed contractors. Understanding how the Personal Allowance (£12,570), progressive income tax bands, and National Insurance (NI) interact is vital for remote professionals based across London, Manchester, Edinburgh, or Bristol.

HMRC Framework for Remote Workers

Remote workers based in the UK generally operate under one of three arrangements:

  • Direct UK Payroll (PAYE): Employed by a UK registered entity or Employer of Record (EOR) with PAYE tax and Class 1 National Insurance deducted at source.
  • Sole Trader / Outside IR35 Contractor: Invoicing foreign or domestic clients via Self Assessment, paying Class 4 National Insurance.
  • Personal Service Company (PSC): Taking a combination of low salary and company dividends, subject to Corporation Tax and Dividend Tax.

The Infamous 60% Marginal Tax Trap (£100,000 to £125,140)

When your adjusted net income exceeds £100,000, your £12,570 Personal Allowance is reduced by £1 for every £2 earned above £100,000, reaching zero at £125,140. This means for every £100 earned in this band, £40 goes to higher-rate income tax, and £20 is added from the loss of allowance, which combined with 2% National Insurance creates an effective 62% marginal tax deduction.

National Insurance Contributions (NICs)

Under recent reforms, the employee Class 1 National Insurance contribution rate on earnings between the Primary Threshold (£12,570/yr) and Upper Earnings Limit (£50,270/yr) is 8%, dropping to 2% on earnings exceeding £50,270.

Bypassing the Trap with Pension Salary Sacrifice

The most effective statutory mechanism to eliminate the 60% tax trap is salary sacrifice into an approved SIPP (Self-Invested Personal Pension) or workplace pension. By sacrificing earnings above £100,000 directly into retirement accounts, you retain 100% of your Personal Allowance while sheltering wealth from income tax and National Insurance.
