## Statutory Tax System Overview (2026)

Under the statutory framework of the Impuesto sobre la Renta de las Personas Físicas (IRPF), international professionals residing in Spain for more than 183 days in a calendar year are classified as domestic tax residents. Spain assesses progressive personal income tax through a bifurcated statutory structure consisting of a state scale (escala general estatal) and an autonomous community scale (escala autonómica). For fiscal year 2026, standard combined tax brackets range from 19% on income up to €12,450 to 47% on earnings exceeding €300,000. Salaried workers benefit from standard statutory deductions including a €2,000 general deductible expense allowance and personal exemptions of €5,550.

### Social Security & Mandatory Deductions

For salaried remote employees, standard social security contributions total 6.50% of gross pay in 2026, comprising common contingencies (4.70%), unemployment (1.55%), professional training (0.10%), and the Mechanism of Intergenerational Equity (MEI) contribution of 0.15% under Orden PJC/297/2026. Employee contributions are subject to a statutory maximum contribution base of €5,101.20 per month (€61,214.40 annually), capping total annual employee contributions at €3,978.94. Conversely, self-employed contractors (autónomos) are subject to the real-income contribution framework enacted by Real Decreto-ley 13/2022. Autónomos deduct a standard 7% generic provision for business expenses, after which net income is mapped across 15 statutory contribution brackets (tramos). For 2026, monthly autónomo payments using the minimum base range from €205.88 for net returns under €670 monthly up to €607.35 for returns exceeding €6,000 monthly, establishing a baseline self-employed annual contribution of €7,288.20 in our model. Autónomos who choose a higher contribution base up to the statutory maximum of €5,101.20 monthly pay up to €1,606.88 monthly (€19,282.54 annually).

### Special Expatriate Tax Regimes

The Spanish Special Expatriate Regime (Beckham Law, Art. 93 LIRPF), expanded under the Startup Act (Ley 28/2022) to encompass international digital nomads and qualified remote employees, allows incoming professionals to be taxed as non-residents on Spanish-source employment income. Under this regime, qualifying individuals are subject to a flat tax rate of 24% on gross earnings up to €600,000, with a 47% marginal rate applying strictly to income exceeding that threshold. This regime completely bypasses standard progressive brackets and exempts non-Spanish investment dividends and foreign capital gains from Spanish taxation for the year of relocation plus the subsequent 5 tax years.

## Frequently Asked Questions: Spain Taxes (2026)

What is the primary qualification criteria for Spain's 24% Beckham Law in 2026?

To qualify for the Beckham Law, you must not have been a tax resident in Spain during the 5 tax years preceding your arrival. You must move to Spain under an employment contract with an employer, under an assignment letter, or as a qualifying international digital nomad holding the Spanish Digital Nomad Visa. An application must be submitted to the Agencia Tributaria (Form 149) within 6 months of registering with Spanish Social Security.

How does Spain tax remote contractors versus remote salaried employees in 2026?

Salaried remote employees contribute 6.50% to Social Security capped at €3,979 annually and deduct €2,000 in general employment expenses. Registered self-employed contractors (autónomos) pay monthly social security cuotas based on 15 real-income tramos. Our model uses the statutory minimum base of each tramo (ranging from €205.88 to €607.35 monthly, or €2,470.56 to €7,288 annually). An autónomo may choose a higher contribution base up to €5,101.20 monthly, resulting in cuotas up to €1,606.88 monthly (€19,282.54 annually). Legitimate business expenses are deductible before applying progressive IRPF rates.

At what gross salary does the Beckham Law become more advantageous than standard IRPF?

Because standard IRPF progressive rates start at 19% and benefit from personal allowances and employment deductions, standard progressive taxation results in lower effective tax below approximately €52,000 to €55,000 for single employees. Above €60,000 gross salary, the 24% flat rate provides substantial savings, expanding rapidly at €100,000 and higher earnings levels.

Does Spain tax my worldwide income if I spend more than 183 days in the country?

Yes. If you spend more than 183 days in Spain during a calendar year without Beckham Law election, you become a standard Spanish tax resident liable to Spanish progressive IRPF on your worldwide income, including offshore dividends, capital gains, and remote overseas business income. Electing the Beckham Law limits taxation primarily to Spanish-source employment earnings.

Are foreign remote contractors eligible for the Beckham Law in Spain?

Under the Startup Act reforms, foreign digital nomads who operate as independent contractors can qualify for the Beckham Law if they hold the formal Spanish Digital Nomad Visa, provided that no more than 20% of their total professional turnover is billed to entities established inside Spain.

## Statutory Tax System Overview (2026)

The Portuguese tax authority (Autoridade Tributária e Aduaneira) assesses individual earnings under a 9-bracket progressive IRS schedule set forth in Article 68 of the CIRS. For the 2026 tax year, statutory rates begin at 12.5% on taxable income up to €8,342 and scale progressively to a top marginal bracket of 48% on income surpassing €86,634. Salaried employees (Category A) benefit from an automatic statutory specific deduction (dedução específica) under Article 25 of the CIRS, established at a baseline of €4,587.09 or the total amount of mandatory social security contributions paid if higher. For independent professionals (Category B) operating under the simplified regime (regime simplificado, Article 31 CIRS), taxable profit is calculated by applying a statutory coefficient of 75% to service income, granting an automatic deemed expense deduction without requiring individual expense receipts.

### Social Security & Mandatory Deductions

For salaried remote workers employed through a local legal entity or Employer of Record (EOR), employee social security is levied at a flat statutory rate of 11% across total gross remuneration, with no statutory maximum earnings ceiling. Conversely, independent contractors registered under Category B contribute at a nominal rate of 21.4% applied to their relevant monthly base of 70% of gross earnings, establishing an effective contribution rate of 14.98% on gross billings. Critically for high-earning remote contractors, independent contributions are subject to a statutory monthly ceiling equal to 12 times the Social Support Index (Indexante dos Apoios Sociais - IAS). Based on the official 2026 IAS benchmark of €537.13 (Portaria n.º 480-A/2025/1), the maximum monthly assessment base is capped at €6,445.56 (€77,346.72 annual base), fixing the maximum statutory annual social security contribution for independent contractors at €16,552.20.

### Special Expatriate Tax Regimes

Following the sunset of the legacy Non-Habitual Resident (NHR) regime for new arrivals, Portugal introduced the IFICI regime (Artigo 58.º-A do Estatuto dos Benefícios Fiscais - EBF). IFICI grants a competitive 20% flat personal income tax rate for a duration of 10 consecutive tax years to qualifying professionals engaged in certified scientific research, high-tech engineering, certified startup ventures, and designated research and development initiatives. To qualify, applicants must establish Portuguese tax residency, must not have been tax resident in Portugal during any of the 5 preceding calendar years, and must exercise an eligible qualified economic activity validated by relevant government bodies.

## Frequently Asked Questions: Portugal Taxes (2026)

What is the new IFICI regime and who qualifies in Portugal for 2026?

The IFICI is Portugal's targeted tax incentive replacing the original NHR. It offers a 20% flat tax rate for 10 years on employment or freelance income derived from qualifying certified activities, including higher education research, certified technological innovation startups, and specialized R&D roles. General remote workers without certified tech/R&D roles are subject to standard progressive IRS rates.

How does Portugal calculate taxes for remote contractors on recibos verdes in 2026?

Under the simplified regime (Category B, Art. 31 CIRS), 75% of your gross professional service earnings is treated as taxable income, while the remaining portion is treated as a deemed business expense. Progressive IRS rates (12.5% to 48%) are then applied to this taxable base. Social security is assessed at 21.4% on 70% of gross earnings, capped at 12 times the IAS (€16,552.20 maximum per year based on IAS €537.13).

Does Portugal offer a social security exemption for new freelancers in their first year?

Yes. Independent workers registering for professional activity with Portuguese Social Security for the first time are eligible for a 12-month exemption from mandatory social security contributions upon request. However, to maintain conservative statutory projections, our baseline calculator models standard recurring contributions.

What is the minimum monthly income requirement for the Portugal D8 Digital Nomad Visa?

The Portugal D8 Digital Nomad Visa requires proof of average monthly income from foreign remote employment or independent contracts equal to at least four times the Portuguese national minimum wage (approximately €3,480 per month in 2026).

Are foreign dividends and pensions tax-free under Portugal's current tax rules?

Unlike the legacy NHR regime which provided zero or 10% taxation on foreign passive income, individuals entering Portugal without IFICI qualification are taxed at standard statutory rates: foreign dividends and interest are generally subject to a 28% flat withholding tax, and foreign pension income is taxed under progressive IRS schedules.
