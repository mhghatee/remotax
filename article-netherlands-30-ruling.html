Netherlands 30% Ruling Tax Guide 2026: Expat Salary Breakdown

The Netherlands remains one of Europe's most prominent technology and engineering destinations. For high-earning foreign professionals recruited from abroad, the Dutch government offers an internationally renowned tax facility: the 30% Ruling (30%-regeling). Under this incentive, up to 30% of gross salary can be paid tax-free as reimbursement for extraterritorial relocation expenses.

How the 30% Ruling Works in Practice

Instead of taxing 100% of your gross compensation under Box 1 progressive income tax rates (which reach 49.5%), your Dutch employer or Employer of Record (EOR) withholds tax on only 70% of your agreed salary. The remaining 30% is disbursed as a tax-exempt allowance, dramatically lifting monthly take-home pay.

Key Eligibility Criteria

To qualify for approval by the Dutch Tax and Customs Administration (Belastingdienst), the applicant must satisfy three statutory tests:

  • Recruited from Abroad: In the 24 months prior to starting employment in the Netherlands, you must have lived more than 150 kilometers from the Dutch border for at least 16 months.
  • Specific Expertise: You possess specialized knowledge or technical expertise not readily available in the local Dutch labor market.
  • Employment Contract: You are employed under a Dutch payroll contract or through an authorized Dutch Employer of Record (EOR).

Statutory Salary Thresholds (2026 Calibration)

To qualify, the taxable salary (i.e., the 70% portion) must exceed minimum statutory levels:

  • Standard Employees: Minimum taxable salary of €46,107 (requiring a minimum gross salary of approx. €65,867).
  • Employees Under 30 with a Master's Degree: Reduced minimum taxable salary of €35,048 (requiring minimum gross salary of approx. €50,069).
  • Balkenende Executive Cap (WNT): The 30% tax-free reimbursement is capped at the maximum public sector remuneration threshold (~€233,000). Earnings above this cap are taxed at ordinary 49.5% brackets.

The Tapering Schedule (30% - 20% - 10%)

Under recent fiscal reforms, the 30% ruling phases down over its 5-year maximum duration:

  • First 20 Months: 30% tax-free reimbursement.
  • Next 20 Months: 20% tax-free reimbursement.
  • Final 20 Months: 10% tax-free reimbursement.

Partial Non-Resident Tax Status (Box 3 Exemption)

Beneficiaries of the 30% ruling historically enjoyed partial non-resident status for Dutch wealth tax (Box 3), exempting foreign stock portfolios and offshore bank savings from Dutch asset taxation. While legislative changes phase out this blanket exemption, existing grandfathered participants maintain protection through their transitional period.
