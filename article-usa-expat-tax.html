The United States is one of only two nations in the world that practices citizenship-based taxation. Regardless of where you live or generate income, all US citizens and permanent residents (Green Card holders) must file annual tax returns with the Internal Revenue Service (IRS). Navigating exclusions and credits is essential to prevent double taxation.

Citizenship-Based Taxation Dynamics

Even if you relocate permanently to zero-tax havens like Dubai or territorial tax systems like Costa Rica, the IRS requires reporting worldwide income on Form 1040. However, two primary statutory mechanisms prevent or significantly reduce double taxation:

  • Foreign Earned Income Exclusion (FEIE / Form 2555)
  • Foreign Tax Credit (FTC / Form 1116)

The Foreign Earned Income Exclusion (FEIE) in 2026

Under Section 911 of the Internal Revenue Code, qualifying individuals living abroad can exclude up to $126,500 of foreign earned income from US federal income tax in 2026. To qualify, you must pass one of two statutory tests:

  1. Physical Presence Test (PPT): Being physically present outside the United States for at least 330 full days in any 365-day rolling 12-month period.
  2. Bona Fide Residence Test (BFR): Being an established tax resident in a foreign country for an uninterrupted period including an entire US tax year (January 1 to December 31).

Foreign Tax Credit (FTC) vs. FEIE: Which is Better?

If you relocate to a higher-tax European nation (such as Spain, Germany, or the UK), the Foreign Tax Credit (Form 1116) is often superior to the FEIE. Since foreign tax paid to countries like Spain or Germany exceeds your US federal tax liability, FTC credits eliminate your US tax bill dollar-for-dollar and allow carrying forward excess credits for up to 10 years.

FICA & Self-Employment Taxes (15.3%)

While the FEIE excludes foreign income from income tax, it does not eliminate FICA self-employment taxes (15.3% SECA) for independent US 1099 contractors unless a bilateral Totalization Agreement applies with your country of residence.

FBAR (FinCEN 114) & FATCA Asset Disclosures

US expats maintaining foreign bank accounts exceeding $10,000 in aggregate balance at any point in the calendar year must file an annual FBAR (Report of Foreign Bank and Financial Accounts) with the US Treasury. Failure to file carries severe civil penalties.
